# AMOS Labs: $100M ARR Execution Strategy

**Planning date:** August 7, 2026  
**Target date:** December 31, 2027  
**Target:** $100M of live, recurring annualized revenue - not pipeline, TAM, pilots, or unsigned expansion.

## Executive decision

AMOS should operate as one horizontal governed operating platform with a focused enterprise wedge:

> AMOS gives distributed organizations one intelligent operating layer for understanding every location or business unit, identifying performance gaps, coordinating governed interventions, delivering relevant learning, and measuring outcomes.

Franchise systems are the best initial wedge because they combine a central buyer, thousands of downstream operating units, measurable performance variation, and a built-in land-and-expand motion. Multi-location corporate networks and multi-business-unit enterprises are the horizontal expansion, not separate products.

The other current initiatives should support the platform without fragmenting it:

- AMOS Enterprise owns the primary revenue engine and receives roughly 70% of product and go-to-market attention.
- AMOS Bio productizes one commercialization-intelligence package around the UT design partnership.
- AMOS Government enters through Nuvola, law-enforcement training, and policy-to-performance workflows.
- The builder, reseller, and SMB motion scales through partners and product-led distribution rather than founder-led sales.

## What $100M ARR actually requires

$100M ARR equals $8.33M MRR. Starting from the deck's August 2026 revenue base, this is a stretch outcome that requires enterprise bookings to lead live revenue by one to two quarters.

The target model is:

| Revenue engine | December 2027 live ARR | Required operating result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Distributed enterprise networks | $70M | 10 parent-network contracts plus 15,000 paid downstream locations at a $250 blended monthly AMOS yield |
| Bio commercialization | $12M | 5 research institutions plus 6-8 life-sciences companies or foundations on repeatable packages |
| Government and real-time learning | $10M | 5 material agency/channel contracts averaging roughly $2M ARR |
| Builder, reseller, and SMB | $8M | Approximately 3,800 downstream customer equivalents at a $175 monthly net AMOS yield |
| **Total** | **$100M** | One platform, four distribution motions |

The $250 downstream-location yield assumes the current $199 entry product plus modest learning, automation, integration, or metered expansion. It should not be presented as achieved until cohort data supports it.

## Neighborly revenue model

Use the following categories consistently:

- **Live ARR:** active recurring service that is currently invoicing.
- **Contracted ARR:** binding, signed recurring payment obligation, even if activation is scheduled.
- **Expansion pipeline:** a mutually understood rollout path without a binding payment obligation.
- **TAM:** the mathematical value of all eligible brands, locations, or franchisees.

An MSA normally establishes legal terms; it does not by itself create $3M of contracted ARR. The $3M belongs in contracted ARR only if an order form, SOW, minimum commitment, or equivalent signed instrument obligates payment. Otherwise, it is high-confidence expansion pipeline.

Assuming 6,000 eligible paid locations and a $199 monthly location price:

| Neighborly state | Corporate ARR | Paid locations | Location ARR | Total ARR |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Pilot conversion | $60K | 0 | $0 | $60K |
| 20% location adoption | $3.0M | 1,200 | $2.866M | $5.866M |
| 40% location adoption | $3.0M | 2,400 | $5.731M | $8.731M |
| 100% location coverage | $3.0M | 6,000 | $14.328M | $17.328M |

Do not build the operating plan on voluntary 100% adoption. The base case should be 20%, the target case 40%, and the stretch case 60%. Achieving 100% normally requires a network-wide corporate license, a mandate, a required-technology designation, or corporate/co-op funding.

### Neighborly must become the repeatable template

The pilot must produce more than a customer logo. It must create:

1. A signed rollout instrument with dates, minimums, and brand sequencing.
2. A reusable organization graph: corporate -> brand -> region -> franchisee -> location -> governed assets.
3. Standard connectors and a canonical performance schema.
4. Before-and-after evidence on a small set of commercial KPIs.
5. A repeatable intervention loop: detect gap -> explain -> recommend -> approve -> train/act -> measure.
6. A franchisee onboarding, provisioning, support, and billing machine.
7. A reference architecture, security package, case study, and deployment timeline that can be reused in the next network.

### Product architecture required by the revenue plan

The revenue plan depends on one reusable governed operating platform—not an LLM
repeatedly reading thousands of location records and not a custom analytics
project for every customer.

The non-negotiable platform invariant is:

> If every LLM provider is unavailable, AMOS continues to ingest approved data,
> calculate official metrics and scorecards, run published automations, route
> approvals, and deliver a usable base report.

| Product capability | Commercial reason | Neighborly proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organization and asset graph | One parent contract can govern brands, franchises, locations, teams, and assets | Corporate-to-location hierarchy supports rollout and rollups |
| Canonical connectors and mappings | The next network is configuration, not a new product fork | Approved source mappings run incrementally across representative brands |
| Deterministic operating runtime | Reliable automation and analysis without inference on every row | Scheduled data-to-scorecard-to-exception-to-report loop runs with models disabled |
| Configurable KPI, scorecard, and initiative engine | Corporate initiatives become reusable product configuration | Exact seven initiatives plus roughly 15-25 component KPIs reconcile to historical outputs |
| Governed intervention and learning loop | Insights produce measurable value and expansion revenue | Detect -> explain -> approve -> act/train -> measure produces one referenceable outcome |
| Versioned performance packs | Deployment effort and FDE hours decline with every network | Second parent pilot reuses most of the Neighborly package |

This is the connective layer between product and revenue: the architecture must
turn Neighborly into a reusable performance pack that can sell into franchises,
corporate multi-location networks, and multi-business-unit enterprises while
preserving enterprise gross margin.

Hardcore system design is maintained separately:

- [Deterministic operating runtime](DETERMINISTIC_OPERATING_RUNTIME.md)
- [Deterministic metrics, scorecard, and initiative engine](DETERMINISTIC_METRICS_SCORECARD_ENGINE.md)

The first Neighborly release should support the exact seven initiatives plus
roughly 15-25 component KPIs, historical backtesting, per-location and roll-up
views, version comparison, evidence drill-down, and exception delivery. The
second release should expose the guarded self-service studio. Later releases
can package anonymized or permissioned benchmark cohorts and reusable vertical
scorecard packs.

### Ideal customer profile

Prioritize organizations with:

- at least 500 locations, operating units, or independently managed business units;
- a central executive sponsor and access to shared data;
- measurable performance dispersion across units;
- authority or economic influence over downstream adoption;
- a workflow where diagnosis can lead to an intervention and a measured result;
- at least $1M of credible parent-level ARR and $3M-$10M of network expansion potential.

Initial vertical order:

1. Home-services and franchise networks.
2. QSR, hospitality, and retail networks.
3. Clinics, dental groups, dealerships, and other multi-site operators.
4. Private-equity multi-unit portfolios.
5. Large multi-business-unit enterprises after the network product is repeatable.

Pizza Hut remains a valuable opportunity, but the account plan must reflect its ownership transition.

On June 16, 2026, Yum announced definitive agreements to sell Pizza Hut outside Mainland China to LongRange Capital and Pizza Hut China to Yum China, with closing expected in Q3 2026. Yum also said it would continue providing Byte by Yum to Pizza Hut outside China during the transition. Source: [Yum transaction announcement](https://investors.yum.com/news-events/financial-releases/news-details/2026/Yum-Brands-Inc--Enters-into-Agreements-to-Sell-Pizza-Hut-for-2-7-Billion/default.aspx).

The resulting account strategy is:

- Treat Pizza Hut/LongRange, Pizza Hut China/Yum China, and remaining Yum brands as distinct buying paths.
- Use the ownership transition as a potential LongRange value-creation opening.
- Preserve the Yum relationship, but do not present Pizza Hut as an automatic cross-sell into every Yum brand.
- Position AMOS above and across operational systems as the governed improvement and learning layer.
- Do not compete head-on with Byte by Yum's POS, ordering, kitchen, inventory, labor, or restaurant-coach functions. Yum describes Byte as its proprietary AI restaurant technology platform, with at least one Byte product already deployed in 25,000 restaurants. Source: [Byte by Yum announcement](https://investors.yum.com/news-events/financial-releases/news-details/2025/Introducing-Byte-by-Yum-an-AI-Driven-Restaurant-Technology-Platform-Powering-Customer-and-Team-Member-Experiences-Worldwide/default.aspx).

The Pizza Hut pilot should prove a complementary loop: combine authorized data across systems, find a performance gap, recommend a bounded intervention, deliver relevant learning, and measure the business result.

## Bio strategy

AMOS Bio should initially sell one narrow, measurable product: a commercialization operating system for research portfolios.

The UT pilot should:

1. Connect invention disclosures, grants, publications, patents, CRM/licensing records, documents, and permissioned communication sources.
2. Backtest 25-100 historical disclosures against known outcomes.
3. Measure surfaced opportunities, partner relevance, review-cycle time, analyst hours saved, and false-positive burden.
4. Move a live portfolio into read-only continuous monitoring.
5. Add governed actions only after authority, confidentiality, retention, export-control, and publication/patent rules are explicit.

Commercial ladder:

- $100K-$250K backtest and pilot.
- $500K-$2M institutional annual deployment based on portfolio and integration scope.
- Expansion into research collaboration graphs, partner discovery, or governed discovery workflows.

Do not promise generalized autonomous science before commercialization intelligence is repeatable.

## Government and real-time learning strategy

The strongest initial government wedge is not a generic AI modernization platform. It is the policy-to-performance loop already supported by Nuvola and the Texas law-enforcement pilot:

1. A policy, operating signal, or readiness gap creates a documented need.
2. AMOS finds or drafts relevant learning.
3. A qualified human approves the course and consequential decisions.
4. Nuvola records assignment, completion, version, and evidence.
5. AMOS measures whether behavior or operating outcomes changed.

Enter through existing learning relationships, primes, resellers, and contract vehicles. Avoid building the 2027 target around long-cycle net-new federal procurement.

Commercial targets:

- $150K-$500K agency pilot or scoped deployment.
- $500K-$2M recurring state, network, or department deployment.
- Training/content production fees remain separate from platform ARR unless contractually recurring.

## Builder, reseller, and SMB strategy

This motion cannot consume the founder's enterprise-sales time. It needs a product and partner machine:

- one-click downstream provisioning and tenant isolation;
- vertical templates and demo environments;
- wholesale pricing with an explicit AMOS net yield;
- partner certification, deal registration, and support boundaries;
- product telemetry for activation, retention, and module adoption;
- payouts tied to collected recurring revenue, not lead volume.

The December 2027 target of $8M ARR requires approximately 3,800 customer equivalents at a $175 monthly net AMOS yield. One workable shape is 30 productive partners averaging 127 active paid customers each. The operating metric is productive partners, not signed affiliates.

## Sales capacity and pipeline requirements

To win 10 parent networks at a 25% qualified-opportunity win rate, AMOS needs roughly 40 genuinely qualified parent-network opportunities. A logo list is not qualified pipeline.

Required operating cadence:

- 50-100 named ICP accounts, ranked by warm access, network size, pain, and ability to mandate or subsidize adoption.
- 15-20 paid pilots initiated through 2027.
- At least 70% pilot-to-production conversion.
- One to two new paid pilots launched each month.
- 3x-4x qualified pipeline coverage against the next four quarters' contracted ARR target.
- Less than 45 days from production signature to first live value.
- Annual prepayment and parent-level minimum commitments wherever possible.

Initial commercial organization:

- Founder remains executive sponsor for the top 10 accounts.
- Enterprise leader owns the repeatable process and forecast.
- Strategic account directors sell parent networks.
- Network rollout managers drive downstream location activation.
- Forward-deployed engineers own integration and time-to-value.
- Customer-success leaders own measured outcomes, references, expansion, and retention.
- Dedicated Bio and Government leaders are added only when each package has a repeatable buyer and implementation.

## Milestone-based hiring plan

The organization should be designed around one reusable platform and small
deployment pods. Do not assign permanent custom engineering teams to individual
customers or staff every business line before its conversion proof exists.

### Wave 1: Neighborly template and enterprise readiness (August-October 2026)

Add approximately 8 critical hires:

| Role | Count | Immediate mandate |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| VP Engineering / Head of Platform | 1 | Own reliability, architecture, delivery cadence, engineering hiring, and enterprise technical accountability |
| Senior data-platform / analytics engineers | 2 | Build canonical contracts, deterministic jobs, recipe runtime, lineage, and location-scale processing |
| Forward-deployed enterprise engineers | 2 | Own Neighborly mappings, integrations, rollout automation, and time-to-first-value while upstreaming reusable components |
| Enterprise product leader | 1 | Turn Neighborly requirements into the distributed-organization core and control custom-scope pressure |
| Enterprise GTM leader | 1 | Build the repeatable parent-network sales process, pricing, pipeline truth, and account plans while the founder remains executive sponsor |
| Network implementation / customer-outcomes leader | 1 | Own pilot success criteria, brand sequencing, downstream activation, change management, and measured outcomes |

Use fractional or contracted security/compliance support immediately, but name
one internal engineering owner for security, SLOs, incident response, data
boundaries, and enterprise diligence.

Wave 1 exit gates:

- Neighborly rollout instrument and data access are signed;
- first production recipe runs across a representative multi-brand/location cohort;
- deployment time, data quality, model cost, deterministic-compute cost, and FDE
  hours are measurable;
- one referenceable measured outcome exists; and
- the next two parent-network pilots use substantially the same architecture.

### Wave 2: Repeatable parent-network delivery (November 2026-March 2027)

Add 12-15 people only after the Neighborly rollout commitment and at least one
additional qualified paid pilot:

- 3-4 additional forward-deployed/data-integration engineers;
- 2 core data/platform engineers;
- 2 reliability, cloud-security, and enterprise-infrastructure engineers;
- 2 strategic account executives;
- 2 network rollout/customer-success managers;
- 1 revenue-operations and deal-desk owner; and
- 1 product designer or product-operations lead focused on operator adoption.

Create an enterprise deployment pod for every two to three active parent
networks: one commercial owner, one technical/FDE owner, and one rollout/outcome
owner. Shared platform, data, security, and product teams serve every pod.

Wave 2 exit gates:

- at least $20M contracted ARR or equivalent prepaid rollout commitments;
- two parent networks in production;
- at least 1,000 paid downstream units;
- median time to first governed insight below 30 days;
- repeatable provisioning and support; and
- forward-deployed effort per new brand is declining.

### Wave 3: Scale by contracted demand (April-September 2027)

Add 20-30 people in tranches tied to contracted ARR and active rollout load:

- expand enterprise pods for additional parent networks;
- grow the core platform/data/AI-learning organization to roughly 18-24 people;
- build a 4-6 person reliability, security, data-governance, and FinOps function;
- add partner/channel operations only after partner-sourced activations are
  repeatable; and
- add dedicated Bio or Government general managers only after each initiative
  has a repeatable buyer, package, reference, and at least $2M-$3M of credible
  contracted ARR.

Capacity ratios should trigger hiring more reliably than the calendar:

- one FDE for every two concurrent parent deployments or major brand launches;
- one rollout manager per 1,000-1,500 locations in active activation cohorts;
- one customer-outcomes lead per $4M-$6M of live enterprise ARR;
- one strategic AE for 8-12 qualified parent accounts and two to three annual wins;
- one platform/data engineering hire whenever reusable backlog threatens the
  next two committed deployments; and
- no segment-specific executive hire without a named pipeline, repeatable offer,
  and funded delivery plan.

At the $100M ARR target, a plausible organization is approximately 55-75 people
if the product and recipes are genuinely reusable. A services-heavy model could
require far more people and would weaken gross margin, speed, and valuation.
With only a $5M-$10M financing, keep the organization closer to 25-40 people,
prioritize Enterprise/Neighborly, and let annual prepayments fund rollout pods.

### Roles that should not be hired too early

- large SDR teams before the founder/enterprise leader has a repeatable message;
- separate engineering organizations for Bio, Government, franchises, and SMB;
- customer-specific data-science teams that do not contribute versioned recipes;
- a large affiliate organization before activation, support, billing, and net
  revenue retention work without manual intervention; and
- management layers without five to eight accountable individual contributors
  or a clear revenue/delivery bottleneck.

## Capital implications

A $5M-$10M round can fund the Neighborly template and several additional networks. It is unlikely to support a fully staffed $100M ARR push across enterprise, Bio, Government, and channel simultaneously unless customer prepayments materially fund delivery.

The current deck's $15M-$20M raise is more consistent with the target, but the plan should still assume:

- annual prepaid contracts and paid implementations;
- milestone-based hiring rather than staffing every division immediately;
- a potential follow-on financing in mid-2027 if contracted ARR is scaling faster than cash collections;
- no custom implementation work hidden inside recurring platform ARR;
- a blended gross-margin target above 75%, including model and inference costs.

## Metrics that run the company

### Revenue truth

- Live ARR, contracted ARR, invoiced ARR, and cash collected.
- ARR by parent platform, downstream unit, module, usage, and services.
- New ARR, expansion ARR, contraction, and churn.
- Gross and net revenue retention.

### Enterprise repeatability

- Paid pilot count and pilot-to-production conversion.
- Days to connect first system, first insight, first governed action, and first measured outcome.
- Forward-deployed engineering hours per launch.
- Parent contract ACV and paid downstream activation rate.
- Paid location CAC, payback, activation, and churn by network.

### Outcome proof

- Customer KPI improvement against baseline.
- Percentage of claims traceable to governed evidence.
- Intervention completion and measured lift.
- Learning assignment, completion, and post-training outcome.

### Platform economics

- Gross margin by model route, workflow, and customer.
- Local/BYOM/managed model mix.
- Cost per successful completed workflow, not token cost alone.
- Support and implementation burden by package.

> Does this make the AMOS governed operating layer more repeatable, easier to deploy, easier to distribute, and more valuable across many organizations?
